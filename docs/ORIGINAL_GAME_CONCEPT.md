# Murim Life — Game Concept

> Working title. A text-based life simulation and cultivation RPG inspired by BitLife and the Murim/cultivation world of *Demonic Emperor / Magic Emperor*.

---

# 1. Core Concept

**Murim Life** is a text-based life simulation RPG where the player lives the entire life of a character inside a dangerous Murim/cultivation fantasy world.

Instead of following a predetermined protagonist, every playthrough creates a new person with a different:

- Family
- Background
- Talent
- Constitution
- Personality
- Luck
- Opportunities
- Enemies
- Relationships
- Cultivation potential
- Destiny

The player begins as an ordinary person within the world and decides what kind of life they will live.

They could eventually become:

- Wandering Cultivator
- Sect Disciple
- Sect Elder
- Sect Master
- Clan Patriarch
- Noble
- Imperial Official
- General
- Merchant
- Alchemist
- Blacksmith / Artifact Refiner
- Assassin
- Mercenary
- Demonic Cultivator
- Righteous Cultivator
- Martial Arts Master
- Criminal
- Bandit Leader
- Hidden Master
- Legendary Ancestor
- World's Most Wanted Cultivator
- Secret mastermind / final villain

There is no requirement to become the "hero."

The player simply **lives a life inside the world.**

---

# 2. Main Inspiration

The game combines two major concepts.

## BitLife

The life simulation structure is inspired by BitLife:

- Aging
- Random life events
- Decisions
- Relationships
- Careers
- Wealth
- Reputation
- Family
- Death
- Multiple generations
- Replayability

However, instead of modern life, the entire simulation takes place inside a Murim/cultivation fantasy world.

## Demonic Emperor / Magic Emperor

The world is heavily inspired by the type of setting found in *Demonic Emperor / Magic Emperor*.

Important inspirations include:

- Powerful cultivation clans
- Noble families
- Sect politics
- Imperial politics
- Betrayals
- Scheming
- Secret techniques
- Ancient inheritances
- Forbidden cultivation methods
- Auctions
- Ancient ruins
- Powerful artifacts
- Rival disciples
- Demonic cultivators
- Righteous sects
- Revenge
- Long-term grudges
- Hidden masters
- Political manipulation

The goal is NOT to recreate the story or characters.

Instead, the game should recreate the feeling of **living inside this type of world.**

# 4. Life Progression

Time advances through player actions and events.

Depending on the situation, time could advance by:

- Days
- Weeks
- Months
- Years

Early childhood may advance primarily through years.

Once cultivation begins, smaller periods become more important.

Example:

> **Age 14 — Blackwind Village**
>
> While collecting herbs outside the village, you discover a severely wounded cultivator.
>
> His robes bear the symbol of the Azure Sword Sect.
>
> He appears unconscious.

Choices:

1. Help him.
2. Search his belongings.
3. Steal his storage ring.
4. Kill him.
5. Report him to the village elder.
6. Leave immediately.

Every decision may have consequences.

---

# 5. Consequences

Choices should NOT simply provide immediate rewards.

A decision made decades earlier may return later.

Example:

At age 17:

> You publicly humiliated Chen Tao during the Outer Disciple Tournament.

The player forgets about it.

Twenty years later:

> **Age 37**
>
> The Azure Sword Sect has issued a kill order against you.
>
> **Reason:** Elder Chen Tao has never forgotten his humiliation.

Characters should remember:

- Insults
- Betrayals
- Favors
- Murders
- Debts
- Friendships
- Romance
- Family relationships
- Saved lives
- Stolen treasures
- Broken promises

This allows the world to develop long-term history.

---

# 8. Cultivation Is Not Just XP

Cultivation should involve more than pressing:

> Cultivate +10 XP

Progress depends on:

- Talent
- Constitution
- Spiritual roots
- Cultivation technique
- Resources
- Pills
- Environment
- Teachers
- Comprehension
- Injuries
- Meridians
- Mental state
- Luck

For example:

The player discovers a Heaven-grade cultivation technique.

However:

> Your constitution is incompatible with the technique.

The player could:

1. Sell it.
2. Give it to their sect.
3. Attempt to cultivate it anyway.
4. Search for a method to modify their body.
5. Find someone compatible with it.
6. Hide it.

Trying to cultivate it could result in:

- Massive power increase
- Meridians being damaged
- Cultivation deviation
- Permanent injury
- Death

# 9. Cultivation Techniques

Techniques can have different grades.

Example:

- Mortal
- Earth
- Heaven
- Spirit
- Divine
- Ancient

Techniques may also have requirements.

Example:

## Nine Infernal Flames Scripture

**Grade:** Heaven

**Affinity:** Fire / Demonic

**Requirements:**

- Fire Spiritual Root
- High Willpower
- Strong Meridians

**Benefits:**

- Increased cultivation speed
- Powerful fire techniques
- Resistance to fire

**Risk:**

High chance of cultivation deviation for incompatible cultivators.

# 10. Combat

Combat should initially remain primarily text-based.

Example:

> **You encounter Zhao Ren.**
>
> Realm:
> Foundation Establishment — Middle Stage
>
> Your Realm:
> Foundation Establishment — Early Stage
>
> Zhao Ren draws his sword.

Possible actions:

- Fight
- Flee
- Negotiate
- Intimidate
- Bribe
- Use artifact
- Use poison
- Call allies

Combat outcomes depend on much more than cultivation realm.

Factors include:

- Realm
- Techniques
- Equipment
- Experience
- Injuries
- Attributes
- Environment
- Preparation
- Intelligence
- Allies
- Luck

This allows weaker characters to defeat stronger opponents through planning.

# 16. Random Events

The event system is the heart of the game.

Events should be generated based on context.

Conceptually:

Age  
+ Realm  
+ Location  
+ Reputation  
+ Sect  
+ Relationships  
+ Traits  
+ Wealth  
+ Previous Decisions  
+ Randomness  

↓

**EVENT**

This means events should usually make sense for the player's current life.

A poor mortal child should not receive the same events as a 400-year-old sect patriarch.

---

# 17. Event Conditions

Example event:

## Ancient Ruins Discovered

Requirements:

- Age > 16
- Cultivation >= Foundation
- Player knows cultivators
- Regional event active

Possible event:

> Rumors spread throughout the Western Province.
>
> Ancient ruins have appeared inside the Black Mountain Range.
>
> Several major sects are already preparing expeditions.

Choices:

- Join your sect's expedition.
- Travel alone.
- Hire mercenaries.
- Ignore the ruins.
- Sell information about the ruins.
- Ambush cultivators returning from the ruins.

The same world event therefore creates multiple playstyles.

# 21. Legacy System

One of the game's defining mechanics.

Previous characters can become part of the world's history.

For example:

Run #1:

The player creates the **Turtor Clan** and becomes its first patriarch.

The character eventually dies.

Several hundred years later...

Run #5:

> You were born into a declining cultivation family.
>
> **Family: Turtor Clan**
>
> Your ancestor was once the infamous Blood Moon Patriarch.

The player could potentially rediscover:

- Previous characters
- Previous clans
- Previous sects
- Previous artifacts
- Previous techniques
- Previous enemies
- Descendants
- Historical events

This means every playthrough contributes to the player's personal world history.

Possible concepts:

# 30. Development Philosophy

The game should NOT begin with hundreds of complicated systems.

The first objective is:

**Make living one Murim life fun.**

Everything else can expand from there.

# 37. Long-Term Vision

The ultimate goal is to create:

> **A life simulator where the player can live thousands of completely different lives inside a persistent cultivation world.**

The player isn't necessarily the chosen one.

They aren't necessarily the hero.

They might become:

A farmer.

A merchant.

A sect disciple.

A legendary cultivator.

A patriarch.

An emperor.

A demonic monster.

A forgotten nobody.

Or someone whose actions permanently change the history of the world.

Every death creates another story.

Every new life begins another chapter.

And over many playthroughs, those lives collectively create the player's own unique history of Murim.

---

# 38. Core Design Rule

When designing any new mechanic, ask:

> **"Does this create an interesting story or decision?"**

If the answer is no, the mechanic probably does not need to exist.

The game's primary reward is not simply:

**NUMBER GO UP.**

It is:

**"What the hell is going to happen to my character next?"**
